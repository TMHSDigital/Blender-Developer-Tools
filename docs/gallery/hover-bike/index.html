Hover Bike

showcase/hover-bike/

A procedural sci-fi hover bike — a sculpted two-tone fuselage with panel seams, gasketed access panels with hex fasteners, a belly service plate, decal plate, hinged charge-port door, heat sink, intake grilles, grab rail and tail light, a stitched pleated saddle, a twin-stanchion fork through sculpted triple clamps with clip-ons, switch pods, pivoted levers and a visored instrument cluster, two glowing exhaust nozzles, two ducted fans with canted stator vanes and handed seven-blade rotors, aerofoil pylons flared into the shrouds with fastener rows and a conduit, and landing gear of twin-plate pressed links and oleo shocks on sixteen pinned joints carrying ski-section skids on wear strips — through UVs, bake, LOD, collider, and Unity glTF, asserting recomputed budgets rather than an API contract.

A showcase piece, not an example, and the second in the vehicles category. It builds a procedural sci-fi hover bike, parked on its landing gear:

  • a sculpted fuselage, lofted from superellipse sections along the bike's length: a muted teal livery canopy with twin pearl racing stripes above a shoulder parting line, a dark composite belly under it, a parting-line groove along both flanks and three transverse panel seams. A tank hump rises ahead of the rider, a tail cowl behind, and nose and tail run as necks into the two shrouds;
  • on the fuselage's skin: intake grilles on the tank flanks, louvred vents on the tail flanks, a crown vent, gunmetal cooling fins under the rider's knee and a finned heat sink near the tail; access panels (each a livery panel proud of a dark gasket by an even 4 mm seam, hex fasteners at its corners), an alloy service plate on the belly, a decal plate (an anodised field on a dark board, no text), a charge-port door hinged along its top edge with an anodised latch, a pillion grab rail arching over the tail and a tail-light cluster (housing, red lamp, dividers) between the exhaust nozzles;
  • a saddle draped over the crown: its underside follows the fuselage a bite inside it, and its top carries puffed pleats between six stitched seams;
  • a cockpit: twin chrome fork stanchions in pleated rubber boots, through two sculpted triple clamps (bosses round both stanchions and the stem, pinch bolts) to anodised fork caps; a steering stem with its nut; clip-on clamps carrying the bars, with switch pods (two buttons each), lever perches, orange levers on chrome pivot bolts, ribbed grips and bar-end weights; an instrument cluster with an emissive screen on a bracket from the top clamp, a small tinted visor rising off its front edge, a low fly screen and a headlight pod on the tank's front slope;
  • two exhaust nozzles leaving the tail flanks over the rear fan, each a cup with a scalloped lip, an orange band, a glowing core and a tail cone;
  • two ducted fans on the centreline, 1.60 m apart. Each shroud is an airfoil-section ring (0.262 m throat, rounded inlet lip, livery outside and dark inside) with an orange band and a marker light, white on the front shroud and red on the rear. Inside, a motor can stands on four canted stator vanes, and a rotor hub, an orange spinner and seven twisted, cambered, lofted blades turn on it. The two rotors are handed;
  • two aerofoil pylons from shroud to shroud, their chord vertical in the fans' downwash, flared into both shroud walls and tied into the belly by two fairing webs each, with a row of hex fasteners along the outer face, a conduit in P-clips along the crest and a ribbed foot peg; and
  • landing gear at each end of each pylon: a twin-plate pressed link from a pylon lug to a skid lug, and an oleo shock (anodised barrel, dark preload collar, pleated boot, chrome rod) from a pylon clevis to the link's middle. Every one of the 16 joints is a headed pin with a nut through two bosses and one eye. The links carry two ski-section skid shoes (flat sole, domed crown, upturned tips) on replaceable wear strips, with bolted anodised end caps and a row of strip bolts, tied by two cross braces with collars.

The layout is solved from named constants. Every blade section is wrapped onto its own cylinder about the fan axis, so the tip is exactly BLADE_TIP from it and the clearance to the throat is a design number (10 mm), not a side effect of a flat section's corners. Grilles, fins, panels and plates are laid on the fuselage's closed-form surface along its own normal. The hull's section parameter is symmetric about its crown, so the hull is its own mirror image vertex for vertex. The gear's pins, bushings, barrels, rods and wear strips carry a part face tag (role and unit), so the joint, shock and ground audits read the parts they mean rather than guessing them from shape.

It asserts budget conformance of the generated result. It does not witness an API contract. "It rendered without error" is not a check.

Composes skills mesh-editing-and-bmesh, bake-high-to-low, depsgraph-and-evaluated-data, engine-export-presets, and snippets bake_normal_high_to_low.py, setup_bake_target_image.py, lod_chain.py / decimate_to_budget.py, convex_hull_collider.py, export_preset_unity.py (helpers copied inline, not imported).

Intended size: 2.23 m long, 0.66 m across the bar ends, 1.11 m to the top of the cluster's visor, with the saddle's top at 0.852 m. The shrouds set the length, the bar-end weights the width and the visor the top. The fuselage is 1.033 × 0.344 m. The origin is under the bike's centre, so it lands on the soles of its wear strips.

The collider is the convex hull of the whole bike. Its count is carried by the two 72-segment shroud lips, which are the hull's outline in plan.

Every falsifier leaves the triangle count at 45796: they move, scale or bend parts, never add or remove them.

DECIMATE COLLAPSE triangle counts are not identical across Blender series, so the LOD gate is a ratio band, not an exact count. Bake pixels are stochastic, so the bake gate is has_data plus operator FINISHED, not byte-identity. Construction uses no RNG; two default runs print identical measurements.

Coplanar pairs are kept at zero by staggering parts, not by widening the band. The two bosses and the eye on a joint are turned to three different phases, so their bores never share a face plane; the two plates of a link and the two cheeks of a clevis differ by 0.4 mm; hex heads are turned 15°, so a row or a column of them never shares a flat; skin, gasket and panel faces sit at least 1 mm apart, front and back.

A ducted fan is only as good as its tip gap: the thrust a duct adds comes from the blade tips running close to the wall. Too wide and it is an open propeller in a ring; the blade touches the wall if the gap is zero. The clearance budget measures that gap along the ray the tip travels on. The coaxiality budget is its precondition, since a rotor off its shroud's axis closes the gap on one side. Blade spacing is the rotor's balance, measured about the rotor's own hub, so a rotor shifted off the shroud axis fails coaxiality alone. A pinned joint binds if its pin is off its bushings' axis, and a shock tops out if its rod is not engaged in the barrel, so the gear carries the same two checks as the loader's rams (showcase/cargo-loader). The densities are named constants (composite shells 1600 kg/m³, the fuselage as a hollow monocoque with its internals 250, aluminium 2700, rubber and lights 1200, leather and foam 300, glass 2500, carbon 1600, chrome-plated steel 7800), and the volumes come from the mesh.

The rear fan is the one --offset-hub, --long-blades and --skew-blade change, because nothing inside a shroud sets the envelope. --offset-hub leaves the tip clearance inside its band and the blade gaps exact, so only coaxiality sees it. --long-blades pushes the tips 2.2 mm into the wall, and every blade still sits in its hub. --odd-hull bows the fuselage by cos(πx / 2·0.515), so both ends stay inside their shroud walls, and it bows everything laid on the skin with it: when only the hull bowed, a panel met a hull facet at a shared plane and the run stopped at hygiene (15) instead. --float-skid lifts one skid's own parts while the other grounds the box; its first draft lifted the skid lug's pin too, which slid a pin face onto a boss's plane and also stopped at 15, so the lug and its pin now stay with the gear. --pop-lens first stopped at 15 because the lens (36 segments) and its housing (32) had faces within 0.6° of parallel; both are now 32 segments turned half a step apart. --narrow-skids keeps both strips on the ground and the envelope unchanged; only the contact polygon shrinks. --offset-pin moves a pin and no bushing; --skew-rod and --short-rod move a rod and no pin, so each fails its own budget alone.

The camera stands high enough to look into both shrouds, where the blades and spinners show. The wall stands 2.8 m behind the bike, and the warm wedge pools on it. Gunmetal and chrome carry a studio reflection term (after showcase/road-bicycle), so the gear reads as metal on the dark stage.
